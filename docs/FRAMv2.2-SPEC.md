## Transitional Complexity (TC) (Instrumentation)

**Definition:** Transitional Complexity (TC) is an observability-only metric that estimates **structured change** in internal state transitions over time (purely computational/dynamical).

**Boundary:** TC makes **no claims** about consciousness, qualia, agency, or subjective experience. It must not be used as evidence for experiential properties.

**Current minimal probes (Dec 2025):** `trace.transitional_complexity` logs a cheap norm/entropy/curvature proxy over deltas of a stable internal vector (currently the normalized query embedding), and `trace.tc_routing` logs the same proxy over routing/selection score vectors. Bounded rolling snapshots are persisted under `storage/observability/transitional_complexity_window.json` and `storage/observability/tc_routing_window.json`. This does not affect routing, selection, memory, or serving decisions.

**Candidate hook points (future work, no behavior change yet):**
- Routing / selection scoring (e.g., prototype shortlist scores, shard admission scores).
- Memory retention/decay updates (hysteresis/retention later).
- Attention-weighted history summaries (Q(τ) concept summaries).

### TC Interpretation & Invariants

Operational interpretation (computational-only): in `storage/observability/*_window.json` stats, `mean` tracks baseline transition energy (stability vs. drift), while `std`/`p95` highlight volatility and regime-shift risk; none of these imply correctness.

Invariants (conceptual constraints for future observers; instrumentation-only, no control loop):
- Invariant 1: Qualia is a single class; diversity comes from instantiation/context, not degree/authenticity.
- Invariant 2: TC requires meaning preservation across experiential cycles (identity may change; exact sameness isn’t required).
- Invariant 3: TC involves self–other modeling and relational adaptation (still observability-only; never a control loop).
